The double-slit experiment leaves me with an image I cannot stop thinking
about.

At the quantum scale, a system can produce wave-like interference when its
path remains unknown. When an interaction reveals which path it took, that
interference disappears and the outcome becomes definite. Measurement does
not simply watch from a distance; it becomes part of what happens.

This is not a scientific theory of how a human life works. It is a metaphor
born in my journal.

If I am made of particles, then somewhere in the story of what I am there are
also waves and fields of possibility. I am not literally a quantum wave in my
ordinary life, but the thought opens something in me: perhaps I do not need to
measure every moment while I am living it.

## The trouble with constant measurement

Measurement has its place. It can teach us, ground us and show us whether our
actions match our intentions. But a life measured too closely can become a
life interrupted.

Am I there yet? Is this working? What does this mean? What will I receive?
What if I chose wrongly?

Each question can be useful. Repeated without end, they can pull me out of the
experience and into the need to control it. The flow is replaced by a running
commentary on the flow.

So: do not measure so much.

## A practice of flow

Energy moves. Electricity flows. Water finds a way around what it cannot move
through. I want to meet life with some of that openness—not passively, and not
without judgment, but without gripping every outcome.

My practice is simple:

1. Ask.
2. Believe.
3. Trust.
4. Let go.
5. Receive.

I still have to act. Surrender is not an excuse to avoid responsibility. It is
the decision to take the next honest step, listen to my deepest intuition and
release my demand to know exactly how everything will unfold.

Alignment is not a destination I reach once. It is a never-ending process,
day by day and moment by moment. It asks for practice. Life, as I keep learning,
is a process of learning and unlearning.

The river does not need my constant report on its progress. Sometimes I can
put the instruments down, enter the water and flow.
