## What it says

Every task competes for attention on two independent axes: how urgent it is and
how important it is. Urgency is about time pressure. Importance is about
consequence. The matrix crosses the two and produces four quadrants, each with
its own verb.

Urgent and important work gets done now. Important but not urgent work gets a
scheduled place in the calendar — this is the quadrant where long-term work
actually lives, and the one that noise crowds out first. Urgent but unimportant
work gets delegated or reduced to the smallest honest version. Work that is
neither urgent nor important gets deleted without ceremony.

The matrix is useful because urgency is loud and importance is quiet. Left
alone, a day fills itself with whatever shouts. Sorting by two axes instead of
one is a small move that changes what a week adds up to.

## Where it comes from

The underlying distinction is attributed to Dwight D. Eisenhower, who is widely
quoted as separating the urgent from the important. Stephen Covey popularised
the four-quadrant form in _The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People_ as the
time-management matrix.
