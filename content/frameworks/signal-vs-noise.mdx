## What it says

Most of what arrives in a day is noise: plausible, urgent-sounding, and
interchangeable. Somewhere inside it is a small amount of signal — the few
things that actually change the outcome. The two are not labelled. Noise does
not announce itself as noise.

The claim of this principle is that signal is not found by looking harder at
everything. It is found by removing things. Focus is not the act of attending
to one thing; it is the act of saying no to a thousand things that are merely
good. Only when the field thins does the shape of what matters become visible
at all.

That is what the visual above argues by demonstration: stare at the full field
and there is nothing to see. Filter it, and a shape was there the whole time.

## Where it comes from

This is a principle associated with Steve Jobs's focus philosophy — most
visibly in how he cut Apple's product line to a two-by-two grid on returning in
1997, and in his repeated insistence that focus means saying no. It is not a
formal published framework, and no exact quotation is claimed here.
