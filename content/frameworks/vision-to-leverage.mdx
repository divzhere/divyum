## What it says

Eight stages, treated as a compounding sequence rather than a linear checklist.
The loop returns wider each time it completes:

1. **Sankalp** — resolve, intention
2. **Abhyas** — consistent practice
3. **Tapas** — disciplined heat, voluntary difficulty
4. **Swadhyaya** — self-study
5. **CBT** — examining and rewiring thought
6. **Proof of Work** — visible, verifiable output
7. **Ishvara Pranidhana** — surrender of attachment to outcome
8. **Building Leverage** — compounding through code, media, capital, relationships

Each stage feeds the next. Resolve without practice evaporates; practice
without difficulty plateaus; output without surrender curdles into anxiety
about results. The spiral form matters: the sequence is meant to be walked
again and again, arriving each time with more capacity than the last.

## Where it comes from

This is Divyum's own synthesis, not a sequence taught in the Yoga Sutras.
It combines resolve and practice with vocabulary drawn from classical yoga,
ideas from cognitive behavioural therapy, and modern thinking about leverage.
For one part of that vocabulary, Yoga Sutra II.1 brings together tapas,
svadhyaya, and ishvara pranidhana; see
[Vivekananda's translation and commentary](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/volume_1/raja-yoga/concentration_its_practice.htm).
The combination here, and the order of its eight stages, are personal. The short
definitions await Divyum's fuller explanation.
